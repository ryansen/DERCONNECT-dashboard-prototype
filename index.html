THE DATA CONTRACT

Inspired by a campus.
Built for experimentation.

DERConnect describes a campus microgrid testbed with HVAC, lighting, photovoltaics, battery storage, and electric vehicles. It does not supply a meter schema or sample readings in the provided paper. This simulation borrows that asset mix; its sizes, schedules, electrical values, and fault cases are modeling assumptions.

Relationships before random noise

Each meter combines scheduled building demand, temperature-sensitive cooling, EV charging, solar production, and constrained battery operation. Shared weather, day-of-week patterns, and correlated noise create repeatable but imperfect daily shapes.

Net import = building load + EV charging + battery charge − PV generation

Battery power is signed: negative means discharge. State of charge has 10–90% limits with 95% one-way efficiency. Net import can be negative. Reactive power and balanced three-phase current are derived consistently, except during deliberately corrupted telemetry.

What the prototype can tell you

“Demand is unusual for this hour and weather” is evidence. “The HVAC is faulty” is a hypothesis. Whole-building meters alone cannot establish equipment-level causes. Component values in the export are simulator-only channels; actual submeter availability must be confirmed.

Resolution has limits

Readings are 15-minute interval averages. They do not represent waveforms, transient faults, harmonics, or protection studies. Voltage and reactive-power channels are optional modeled telemetry; confirm meter capabilities before treating them as required fields.

Known limitations

The generator has no empirical DERConnect calibration, tariff model, feeder power flow, equipment dynamics, holiday calendar, or real labeled faults. Weather is synthetic and the meter arrangement is invented. Use it for interface and pipeline experiments, then validate assumptions on authorized field data.

Context changes the interpretation

Recurring after-hours episodes, persistent sub-threshold sequences, and coincident telemetry gaps across meters provide additional evidence. The history rules use observed power, time, and the learned baseline; they never read injected labels or hidden component values.

Red marks high priority; yellow marks medium. Priority indicates review urgency, not a confirmed equipment fault or electrical safety classification. Related episodes and earlier evidence remain visible in the investigation.

A deliberately simple baseline

Fit weekday/weekend 15-minute slot medians and a pooled within-slot temperature correction using only August 24–September 20. Scale residuals with a robust MAD estimate and a 7% / 3 kW floor.

Freeze per-meter thresholds at the validation 99.5th percentile, with a minimum score of 3. Validation spans September 21–October 4 and includes three injected events; calibration does not read their labels.

Demand deviations, missing intervals, eight unchanged readings, and power factor below 0.75 with unusually high reactive power generate separate alert types. Thresholds are illustrative prototype settings, not electrical operating limits.

Training, validation, and test are chronological. The dashboard's expected line is a learned estimate; it is different from the clean counterfactual.

Point and pattern behavior guide the injection catalog: spikes, contextual after-hours loads, and drift. Flatlines and gaps add practical telemetry cases.
